# Why are we here

## 1. Summary

Public examples: The turbulent AI era is here. The choices we make now are critical.
We need a plan to ensure that the good outweighs the bad. By Bill Gates, published on Wednesday, Aug 26, 2026
Ideation: Generating and testing a wide range of ideas, guided by what we’ve learned from real-world contexts.
Implementation: Bringing solutions to life through rapid prototyping, iteration, and collaboration with partners, organizations, and communities.

## 2. Describing vs. Specifying

The Limit of Prompting: Natural language describes feelings, but code specifies precise dynamic logic, animation easing, and responsive thresholds.
Concrete Example: Requesting an "unsettling interactive poster" generates generic templates. True emotional depth requires fine-tuning Bézier curves, GPU frame rates, or non-linear mouse acceleration.

## 3. Materiality & Rapid Iteration (IDEO Framework)

Inspiration to Implementation: Integrate IDEO's (n.d.) methodology—human interaction uncovers unmet needs, but code gives designers the capacity for rapid prototyping and iteration.
Code as Material: Just as painters understand paint, code is the digital artist's raw material. Without computational thinking, designers remain bound by AI's default parameters.

## 4. The Designer as Critical Reader & Director

Reading vs. Writing: AI can write 80% of boilerplate code, but designers must possess the literacy to evaluate, debug, and optimize performance.
Historical Context: Drawing on historical parallels (e.g., Frieder Nake in 1965), technology doesn't replace artists; it shifts their role to critical directors who push the medium beyond automated conventions.

## 5. Conclusion: Retaining Agency

Reiterate that programming knowledge isn't about competing with AI's speed, but about mastering the tools that mediate our artistic perception.
